## GPT-6

The strategy is clear and unusually disciplined. I can identify exactly where Fable will play: with existing premium users who have come through a stressful period, particularly those still engaging with sleep content between days 60 and 120. The how-to-win is equally clear: use Fable’s knowledge of what helped each person to create a personal, voluntary nightly wind-down that supports lasting progress. The refusal to build community, social sharing or daily streaks is explicit and well reasoned. The strongest strategic insight is recognising that some churn is healthy and that Fable must not manufacture dependency to retain customers. However, there is still some wish-list thinking in the proposed moat: fewer users returning to acute-level use is an aspiration, not yet demonstrated competitive advantage. The self-check, fast route back, buddy experiment and freemium upgrade also represent several possible future strategies competing for attention.

The roadmap, charter and stakeholder message largely follow from the strategy. The concierge experiment is particularly strong: it tests genuine demand before committing engineering resources, incorporates consent and clinical safeguards, and has explicit stop conditions. The charter establishes meaningful ownership and decision rights, while the Growth message translates the hard no into a practical working relationship rather than simply rejecting another team’s priorities. The business case is the weakest element. The headline economics are attractive, but the assumptions need more scrutiny. In particular, 20,000 monthly-plan deep users per cohort, a 20-percentage-point retention improvement and $155 lifetime margin per additional retained customer combine into a very large return. The presentation does not sufficiently demonstrate that these assumptions are independently credible. There is also a tension between the 35% to 55% KR1 ambition and the 45% minimum kill threshold: the latter could allow a materially underperforming initiative to continue.

My one change would be to add a single, explicit evidence-to-investment bridge between the concierge experiment and the full build. Show precisely what the experiment must demonstrate, what remains unproven, and which investment decision follows from each result. In particular, separate evidence of engagement, willingness to pay and sustainable day-180 retention. These are three different things, and success at one does not establish the others. Make the financial case conditional on proving each stage, rather than allowing the projected $7.4M return to make the underlying assumptions feel like established facts.

Needs another pass — the product strategy and leadership decisions are strong, but the financial case and the thresholds governing continued investment need to be reconciled before board submission.
