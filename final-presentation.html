Fable Growth: the app people keep after the hard stretch

Fable bets its one initiative on the people it helped through a hard stretch: a personal nightly wind-down keeps them once they recover, and the community layer waits.

The case

Six deliverables, one strategy

Each module added one piece: the strategy, the roadmap, the team that owns it, the message that gets it backed, the business case, and what you learned building it.

Module 1
Product strategy
Where you play and how you win

Winning aspiration: When the hard stretch passes, people keep Fable to hold on to the progress they made, sleeping well and staying steady, because it knows what helped them and has it ready the moment they reach for it.
Where to play: Adults who used Fable heavily through a stressful stretch and still use its sleep content as their need eases (about day 60–120), reached inside the app (churned deep users only by a Growth email inviting them to the concierge test), premium users first. Out: competing with Headspace and Calm for new users, selling to employers, and anyone needing clinical care, whom we signpost elsewhere.
How to win: Fable is the only app that saw what actually helped each person through their hardest stretch. We use that history to fit each person's steady plan, built around a nightly wind-down, from the first night, and a self-check the user starts brings back what worked. Over time, evidence that fewer Fable users drop back into acute-level use can become the moat rivals can't copy quickly.

The hard no

We will not build a community and social layer (no posting between users, groups or profiles), because it serves a different job from staying steady, brings moderation and safety risk with users at vulnerable moments, and would split a team of 14 before the post-acute bet is proven.

Objective and key results

Objective: Make Fable the app people keep after the hard stretch passes.
KR1: Monthly-plan premium users who engaged deeply in their first 90 days and are still subscribed at day 180: 35% → 55% for cohorts reaching day 90 by month 12, read by month 15.
KR2: Those users using a sleep or wind-down session at least three nights a week from day 90 to 180: 15% → 40% for the same cohorts.
KR3: Those users who say in an in-app survey at day 180 that Fable helps them stay steady without feeling dependent on it: 40% → 70% for the same cohorts.

Module 2
Outcome roadmap
Now, next, and later

NOW (0 to 3 months)

▸ Bet 1 (KR3, KR1): We bet a concierge steady plan, a nightly wind-down the content team hand-builds from what helped each person, will show the post-acute need is strong enough that people use it and pay for it, for a small consenting group of churned and current day 60–120 deep users, before we build it for monthly-plan premium users at day 60–120. Owners: PM 2, Content (hand-building the plans), Clinical advisory, Design, Growth (the invite to churned users). Signal: of at least 500 churned and 400 current users invited, at least 30% of each opt in; at least 35% of participants use the wind-down three or more nights a week by week 6, at least 15 points above a matched holdout that also gets the reminder and load-time fixes; at least 10% of the 150 or more churned participants resume a paid plan at the existing price; and a regulatory opinion confirms self-checks fall outside device rules. If not, the build stops and the unspent budget returns to the board.

▸ Bet 2 (KR2): We bet wind-down reminders that land at bedtime in the user's own timezone will make the nightly wind-down a habit instead of a ping people learn to ignore, for post-acute premium users. Owners: Engineering, PM 2, Growth (lifecycle messaging). Signal: users with 3+ wind-down nights a week rise from 15% to 22% and opt-outs fall by 30% relative to today, with at least 98% of reminders landing within 15 minutes of bedtime in local time as the delivery guardrail.

▸ Bet 3 (KR2): We bet a wind-down that opens in under 2s on 4G will stop people giving up at bedtime before the session starts, for post-acute premium users. Owners: Engineering. Signal: drop-off between opening the app and starting a session halves, and session starts per reminder rise by at least 20%.

NEXT (3 to 6 months)

▸ Steady plan v1 for the monthly premium cohort, rules-based from each user's session history, with AI personalisation only once v1 lifts retention. It comes after Now because the concierge test must prove demand and the regulatory opinion must clear first. Owners: PM 2 (lead), Design, Engineering, Content (re-sequencing the library). Signal: day-180 retention for the monthly cohort tracking towards 55%, with at least 45% for the first two monthly cohorts to reach day 180 on v1, and KR2 at 30% and KR3 at 50% for the same cohorts.

▸ Self-checks the user starts that bring back what worked. They need a live steady plan to point to, plus clinical sign-off. Owners: PM 2 (lead), PM 3, Clinical advisory, Design. Signal: at least 40% of plan users start a self-check in their first month, with no clinical-safety incidents at the monthly review.

▸ Consent and privacy model for using wellbeing history at scale. It's needed before personalisation goes beyond the concierge group, which consents person by person in Now. Owners: Legal/Privacy (lead), Engineering. Signal: consent opt-in at 70% or more.

▸ Care-signposting partner live. It must be in place before any self-check can show that someone needs more than Fable. Owners: Clinical advisory (lead), Product. Signal: live before the first self-check ships.

▸ Extend to annual-plan users. Only after the monthly cohort shows a retention lift, because KR1 measures monthly users only. Owners: PM 2 (lead), Engineering. Signal: renewal at month 12 above today's annual rate.

LATER (6 to 12 months)

▸ Evidence that fewer users drop back into acute-level use. A potential moat, not yet one: it needs 12+ months of cohort data against a control group, and a clinically informed, non-diagnostic definition of acute-level use. Owners: Data/Insights (lead), Clinical advisory. Signal: a statistically significant gap in acute re-entry between steady-plan users and a control group.

▸ A fast route back. When a self-check shows a dip, offer a one-tap return to the content that helped last time, before the need becomes acute again, with signposting to care where needed. Owners: PM 2 (lead), PM 3, Clinical advisory. Signal: users return to a session within 48 hours of a dip, and KR3 at 70%.

▸ A gated buddy experiment. One-to-one accountability with someone the user already knows, run only if KR1 plateaus once the steady plan is live. Owners: PM 2 (lead), Growth. Signal: pairs retain at least 10 points better than solo users.

▸ The steady plan as an upgrade path from free. Post-acute freemium users get a taste of the steady plan. Owners: Growth (lead), PM 2. Signal: free-to-premium conversion from the steady plan at least 2x the baseline.

Trade-off memo

Rock 1: The steady plan and the self-check the user starts, reframed from the backlog's AI-generated daily check-in prompts so the user starts every check and AI waits until v1 lifts retention (supports KR3 and KR1). It draws on what helped each user before, so they feel steady through their own plan rather than chased by Fable; a concierge test comes first, with the content team hand-building plans for a small consenting group of churned and current users, and a regulatory opinion before launch.
Rock 2: Fix the notification system (wrong times, timezone bug) (supports KR2). The nightly wind-down reminder has to land at bedtime in the user's own timezone or it trains people to ignore it, and wrong-time pings are what drive the opt-outs the guardrail watches.
Rock 3: Reduce app load time from 4.2s to under 2s on 4G (supports KR2). At bedtime a 4.2-second load is a reason to give up; under 2 seconds keeps the wind-down effortless for every user.
Hard no 1: Launch a premium tier with therapist-matching. It moves Fable towards the clinical positioning it can't take, serves the acute phase rather than the post-acute user, and is the biggest build on the list.
Hard no 2: Build a social layer: share your progress with friends. Sharing mental-health progress with friends puts people at risk when they're most vulnerable, however it's staffed, and would split a team of 14 before the steady plan is proven; a one-to-one buddy experiment runs only if KR1 plateaus once the steady plan is live.
Hard no 3: Add a daily streak feature to drive habit formation. It rewards daily opens, the kind of nudging that leaves people feeling chased, and works against KR3's "without feeling dependent"; the wind-down counts nights a week, not unbroken days.

Module 3
Team charter
What we own

We own: The post-acute experience from day 60 to day 180: the steady plan (hand-built concierge plans now, rules-based v1 next), the nightly wind-down and its load time, the bedtime reminder jointly with Growth, and the self-checks users start themselves, with PM 2 accountable for KR1–KR3. PM 1 owns days 0–60, including the first time a user is shown the wind-down, and PM 3 owns the AI check-in layer as a shared platform, but what that layer shows a user after day 60 is PM 2's call.
Out of scope: New-user acquisition, paid and app-store marketing, and all lifecycle messaging other than the bedtime reminder (win-back emails, re-engagement campaigns) belong to Growth. Diagnosis, urgent support and clinical care belong to Clinical advisory and the signposting partner; consent and data policy belongs to Legal/Privacy; and employer or insurer offers belong to Commercial and stay out of scope until post-acute retention and willingness to pay are proven.
Joint calls: Before anyone builds or ships, PM 1 and PM 2 jointly agree any change to days 0–60 that affects what the steady plan can use, and PM 2 and Growth jointly agree any message to a deep user in the three hours before bedtime. Any new self-check prompt, trigger or wording needs PM 3, PM 2 and Clinical advisory to sign off together; Fable never starts a check-in, and AI personalisation waits until steady plan v1 shows a retention lift. The share of Growth, Legal/Privacy and Data/Insights time the initiative gets is agreed with their leads each quarter.

How we decide

Who decides scope: Within the roadmap, the PM who owns the metric a feature moves decides its scope without needing approval: PM 1 for activation, PM 2 for KR1–KR3 and PM 3 for check-in accuracy and response time. Clinical advisory can veto anything that could read as diagnosis or monitoring, and the engineering lead decides how and when work is built, not whether. Only the Fable product lead can move work between Now, Next and Later or add a fourth Now bet; a kill criterion such as Bet 1's stop rule can only be lifted by a new business case to the board.
How cross-team conflicts escalate: Either party raises the conflict within two working days of spotting it, in a one-page decision note (the options, the KR each affects, and their recommendation), and the people involved have three working days to settle it. If they can't, the Fable product lead decides within two working days of reading the note, or the CPO does when Growth is a party, using one test: which option better protects day-180 retention for deep users without crossing the clinical line. The decision is logged and everyone commits to it; a conflict about clinical safety goes to the product lead and the clinical lead the same day, and the safer option holds until they decide.

Module 4
Stakeholder message
The hard no

Hard no: We are not building a community and social layer or progress sharing.
Stakeholder: The Growth team lead

The message

Growth co-owns the bedtime reminder we ship this quarter, and any message we send a deep user in the three hours before bedtime is a joint call between you and PM 2. We are not building a community and social layer or progress sharing, and that decision is made; the daily streak is off the plan too. The only social work on the roadmap is a one-to-one buddy test, led by PM 2 and co-designed with you, run only if day-180 retention stays flat for two monthly cohorts after the steady plan launches; I make that call. I know you pushed for social features and streaks to keep people longer and grow through referrals, and this takes them away while your targets stay the same. The reason is the people we serve: even fully staffed, sharing mental-health progress puts people at risk when they're most vulnerable. On Thursday I'll meet you and PM 2 to agree the reminder plan and how we'll measure a referral invite Growth can run this quarter that shares nothing about anyone's use, and you'll get the day-180 numbers at each monthly cohort review.

Module 5
Business case
The strategic bet

Recovered premium users who still use sleep content keep a nightly wind-down built from what helped them, so more of them are still paying at day 180. Each one kept is about $155 of margin that Fable now loses when the hard stretch ends.

The assumptions, ranked

1. People who have recovered want an ongoing plan and will keep paying for it (the concierge test's opt-in and paid return).
2. A rules-based steady plan keeps most of the concierge test's lift once it's automated. The hand-built plans set the upper limit, so the concierge group is measured against a matched holdout that also gets the reminder and load-time fixes; if automation keeps only 70% of the lift, the case still returns about 2.3x.
3. About 20,000 monthly-plan deep users reach day 90 each month, so a 20-point lift means about 4,000 more paying at day 180 per cohort (est.; checkable in Fable's data now).

The expected return

Per customer: Each extra monthly-plan user still paying at day 180 is worth about $155 of margin: $12 a month, about $8.40 after app-store fees and running costs, for about 2 more months to day 180, then about 17 at 6% monthly churn (est.).
Volume target: 4,000 more monthly-plan users still paying at day 180 in each monthly cohort once the steady plan is at full strength (KR1 from 35% to 55% for cohorts reaching day 90 by month 12): about $7.4M of lifetime margin per year of cohorts against about $2.3M a year for the team of 14 and regulatory and partner costs, or about $0.3M of cash in year one and break-even around month 22 (est.).

The kill criterion

By the end of month 3, if fewer than 30% of either invited group opts in (at least 500 churned and 400 current users), fewer than 35% of participants use the wind-down three or more nights a week by week 6 or that share is less than 15 points above a matched holdout, fewer than 10% of the 150 or more churned participants resume paying at the existing price, or the regulatory opinion doesn't clear self-checks, we stop the steady plan build, keep only the bedtime reminder and load-time fixes, and return about $1.7M of Next and Later budget to the board; any further investment needs a new business case. If the build goes ahead and day-180 retention is below 45% (10 points of lift, about 1.6x; break-even is 41%) for the first two monthly cohorts to reach day 180 on the steady plan, the same applies to the remaining budget.

Module 6

What I learned

Where the strategy was hardest to write, what you will keep doing, and the moment your thinking changed.

Friction

The hardest part was testing demand without harming the people I was testing. My first plan was a fake door, which would have disappointed vulnerable users and used their data without consent, so I replaced it with a concierge test: steady plans hand-built for a small group who agreed to take part, with a stop rule that returns the budget to the board if it fails.

Key learnings

I will lead a hard no with the risk to the people we serve, not our capacity, because a capacity reason invites an offer of capacity. I will write the kill line before a bet starts, with a metric, threshold, deadline and consequence that nobody who benefits from the bet can lift. And when one decision changes, I will read across every decision that rests on it, with a second model as a cheap reviewer.

Aha moment

When both models asked whether people who have recovered want an ongoing relationship at all, I saw that some of Fable's churn is healthy. That moved the strategy from spotting dips to a nightly wind-down people choose, and made KR3 ask whether users feel steady without feeling dependent, so Fable can't win by keeping people anxious.
