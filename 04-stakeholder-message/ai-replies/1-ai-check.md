### Reading this as the Growth team lead

My reaction: I understand you’ve said no, I understand why, and I can see where I still have influence. But I might feel that Growth is being given responsibility for retention without being given the influence it wanted over the solution.

#### 1. Could I read this as a maybe?

Mostly no. You are explicit:

“We are not building a social layer, sharing progress with friends included, in this initiative, and that decision is made.”

That’s clear and difficult to misinterpret.

However, there is one phrase that leaves a small opening:

“in this initiative”

As Growth, I could interpret that as an invitation to bring the idea back in the next initiative or argue for a smaller experiment within the current one.

If that is intentional, fine. If the decision is final for this initiative, I would leave it as written. If you mean permanently, you need stronger wording.

#### 2. Does it acknowledge what I need and what this costs me?

Yes, but it could go further.

This is particularly good:

“I know a social layer has been Growth’s answer to retention from the start, and that this, with the daily streak, takes two of your asks off the plan and the referral loop that came with them.”

You recognise that this isn’t just rejecting a feature. You are rejecting a strategic direction Growth has advocated and removing potential acquisition and referral opportunities.

However, you don’t explicitly acknowledge the commercial ambition behind those requests.

As Growth lead, what I actually want is:

- More users retained beyond 90 days.
- More opportunities for organic acquisition.
- A measurable contribution to growth.
- A meaningful say in how we achieve those outcomes.

You acknowledge what I’ve lost, but not quite what I was trying to achieve.

#### 3. Is the path forward specific?

Yes. This is the strongest part of your message.

Three things work particularly well:

- Ownership: Growth co-owns bedtime reminders.
- Decision rights: Messaging before bedtime is a joint call between Growth and PM 2.
- Visibility: I’ll see the day-180 retention numbers at the same time you do.

The proposed meeting also has a clear purpose, participants and timing.

One small concern: you say you’ll book an hour, but don’t specify when. That’s easily resolved.

I’d also be reassured that Growth isn’t simply being asked to execute someone else’s retention strategy.

---

#### My one recommended change

I’d strengthen the acknowledgement of the commercial objective and make the alternative feel like a genuine opportunity to influence the outcome.

For example, replace:

“I know a social layer has been Growth’s answer to retention from the start…”

With:

“I know you’ve been pushing for social, streaks and referrals because you see them as ways to improve retention and create organic growth. I’m taking those opportunities off this initiative, and I recognise that’s a significant change to the growth approach you’ve been advocating.”

Why this is better:

1. It acknowledges the commercial rationale, not just the features.
2. It recognises the cost of the decision.
3. It avoids implying that Growth’s ideas were misguided.
4. It makes the disagreement about strategic prioritisation rather than departmental preferences.

#### Final stakeholder verdict

| Question | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Is the no clear? | Yes. |
| Does it acknowledge my position? | Yes, although the commercial ambition could be clearer. |
| Is the rationale credible? | Yes. Team capacity and safeguarding are concrete constraints. |
| Do I retain meaningful influence? | Yes, through reminders, messaging and shared metrics. |
| Is there a clear next step? | Yes. |

One final observation: Your best sentence is the one giving Growth joint ownership of bedtime reminders. It demonstrates that saying no to a proposed solution doesn’t mean saying no to the stakeholder’s contribution.

That’s the distinction that keeps a difficult product decision from becoming a departmental conflict.

#### 1. Could I read this as a maybe? Yes, in two places.

- "in this initiative": As Growth lead, I read this as "not now, but maybe in the next initiative". So I wait for the next budget round and ask again.
- "while we prove the steady plan": This tells me what to wait for. Once the plan is proven, my ask comes back.
- What the message leaves out: Your roadmap has a gated buddy experiment, and I know it's there. Because you don't mention it, the no sounds partly open. You also just moved its lead from Growth to Product, so I'll suspect it's being taken from me quietly.
- "that decision is made" is firm, but the two phrases above weaken it.

#### 2. Does it acknowledge what I need and what this costs me? It names my features, not my problem.

- What works: You name the social layer, the streak and the referral loop, and you admit social has been Growth's answer from the start. That feels fair and specific.
- What's missing: The real cost is that I've lost my main levers for referrals and acquisition. My targets haven't changed, and you say nothing about how I'm supposed to hit them.
- The opening reads like a consolation prize. Starting with co-owning the bedtime reminder before saying no comes across as softening me up, and I'll notice. A reminder I co-own doesn't replace a growth loop.

#### 3. Is the path forward specific? Only for your plan.

- The meeting is vague. "This week, an hour, with PM 2" has no date and no defined outcome.
- The day-180 promise is months away. Seeing day-180 retention "at the same time I do" sounds collaborative, but it gives me nothing to act on this quarter.
- There's no path for my goal. I don't know what Growth can do for referrals within the rules, or what evidence would reopen social.

One change: replace the open-ended scope with an explicit condition.

Cut "in this initiative" and "while we prove the steady plan", and say exactly what is and isn't on the table:

"We are not building a social layer or progress sharing this year. The only social work on the roadmap is a one-to-one buddy test, Product-led, which runs only if day-180 retention stalls once the steady plan is live, and Growth will co-design it."

This removes both maybes by naming the single condition under which social comes back. It also tells me what happened to the buddy experiment before I find out on my own, which protects the relationship more than any acknowledgement line.

If you can make a second change, add one sentence about referrals. For example, offer to agree a privacy-safe referral route in the same meeting. Without that, I leave the meeting with my number intact and none of my tools.
