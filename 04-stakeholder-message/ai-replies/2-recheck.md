### Reading this as the Growth team lead

My reaction: This is considerably better. I understand the decision, I understand the commercial trade-off, and I can see that I still have meaningful ownership. However, you’ve introduced one ambiguity that could undermine your otherwise firm decision.

#### 1. Could I read this as a maybe?

The main decision is very clear:

“We are not building a community and social layer or progress sharing, and that decision is made.”

There is little room to negotiate that.

However, I would question this sentence:

“The only social work on the roadmap is a one-to-one buddy test, led by PM 2 and co-designed with you…”

As Growth, I could interpret this as an invitation to keep pushing for social functionality through the buddy experiment.

There is also an undefined condition:

“which runs only if day-180 retention plateaus once the steady plan is live.”

What constitutes a plateau? Who decides? When?

You’ve introduced a legitimate future experiment, but haven’t defined its decision criteria.

Verdict: The strategic no is clear. The exception needs tighter boundaries.

#### 2. Does it acknowledge what I need and what this costs me?

Yes. This is the strongest improvement over your previous message.

In particular:

“I know you pushed for social features and streaks to keep people longer and grow through referrals, and this takes them away while your targets stay the same.”

This is excellent stakeholder management.

You acknowledge three things:

- The commercial rationale behind my proposal.
- The fact that you’re removing things I’ve advocated for.
- The uncomfortable reality that I’m still accountable for the same targets.

That last point matters enormously.

As Growth lead, I might still be frustrated because you’re removing potential growth levers without adjusting my expectations.

However, I would feel that you understand the consequences of your decision rather than simply dismissing my proposal.

The referral alternative is also important:

“a referral invite Growth can run this quarter that shares nothing about anyone’s use”

You’re not just taking something away; you’re offering an alternative that addresses part of the original growth ambition without compromising user privacy.

#### 3. Is the path forward specific?

Yes, and considerably more actionable.

You establish:

| Area | Ownership / commitment |
|---|---|
| Bedtime reminders | Growth co-owns |
| Pre-bedtime messaging | Joint decision between Growth and PM 2 |
| Buddy experiment | PM 2 leads, Growth co-designs |
| Referral initiative | Growth runs this quarter |
| Retention visibility | Shared access to day-180 numbers |
| Next discussion | Thursday, with you, Growth and PM 2 |

This is good because I leave the conversation knowing where I can contribute and what happens next.

One minor concern: the referral invite is an activity, not yet an outcome. I’d want to agree how its success will be measured.

---

#### My one recommended change

Clarify the boundary around the buddy experiment.

I’d replace:

“The only social work on the roadmap is a one-to-one buddy test, led by PM 2 and co-designed with you, which runs only if day-180 retention plateaus once the steady plan is live.”

With:

“The community and social layer is out of scope. The only exception we may consider is a separately gated one-to-one buddy experiment, led by PM 2 and co-designed with you, if day-180 retention plateaus after the steady plan launches; that decision will require a separate review.”

This does three things:

1. Makes clear that the original decision is not being reopened.
2. Positions the buddy experiment as a conditional future investment, not an alternative route to delivering social features.
3. Establishes that further investment requires another decision.
